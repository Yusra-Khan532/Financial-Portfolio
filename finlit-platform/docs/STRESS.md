* **Covered:** large and mid caps (market cap 10,000 Cr or more) that are not banks, NBFCs, insurers or other financial companies (the screener table and NSE's sector decide, `service.coverage_reason`), with three years of results. Others get a 404 `not_covered` whose message the page shows as it is. Rebuild `launch-universe-*.csv` on launch day.

* **The value move** is a percentage and never a price. `STRESS_SHOW_VALUE=0` removes it from every answer (`flags.showValue` tells the page).
* **Latest-quarter check and inventory days** (task 79): `page.latest_quarter_check()` replays the latest reported quarter against the same quarter a year earlier (crude, rupee and inflation averaged over each quarter, volume from revenue less WPI) with the company's own settings, and sets the margin it gives beside the margin reported (Asian Paints: 17.6% before, 13.9% model, 15.6% reported). ## How the nightly run behaves (12 Nov 2026 fix)

- A company whose report or statements are not stored yet, or whose statements the data provider answers slowly, is **waiting**, not a fault: it is recorded in `stress_failures` (kind `not_ready` or `failed`, with the reason) and tried again on the next run. These never fail the job.
- The run exits with an error only when it tried at least 10 companies and built none (the provider or the macro inputs are down); the supervisor then retries with its back-off. Every other run exits cleanly and the next run continues where this one stopped, so the job never stops after a few failed attempts.
- Three companies are built at the same time (`CONCURRENCY`), because the provider's statements are the slow part. Companies that failed in the last 6 hours go to the back of the queue, so each run first spends its time on companies not tried yet.
- Each run writes one log line, `stress run: covered N, built N, not covered N, not ready N, failed N, left N ...; reasons {...}`, and the reasons per company are in the table:
  `docker compose exec db psql -U finlit -d finlit -c "select kind, left(reason,90), count(*) from stress_failures group by 1,2 order by 3 desc"`.

## Pages are built on demand too (12 Nov 2026, evening)

The nightly `stress` job waits its turn behind the other bulk jobs, and every deploy restarts the app (which stops a running job), so on a busy day it may not reach every company. The pages no longer depend on it alone:

- **A visit builds the page.** When a company has no stored page, the visit starts a build (or joins the one already running; one build per company) and waits at most `LIVE_BUILD_S`. If that is not enough the reader gets the plain "being prepared" card, but the build is **not cancelled**: it carries on (limit `PER_COMPANY_S`) and stores the page. Before this, a slow data provider meant the build was cancelled at 8 seconds and the page stayed "being prepared" for ever.
- **The page checks again by itself** every 20 seconds for about five minutes, so it appears without a reload.
- **The start page warms the largest companies**: while fewer than six quick picks are ready, opening the start page starts background builds for the 12 largest covered companies that have no page (at most three at a time), and the quick picks look again every 30 seconds.
- Failures still go to `stress_failures` with the reason, and the nightly job still builds every company.

## Every covered company gets a page (13 Nov 2026, task 79 backend)

What still stopped pages after PR 361 and 362, and what changed:

- **The rupee base average counts months, not points (SL-1).** `/api/macro` `usdinr` holds monthly averages to Sep 2026, October's average, then the daily FBIL rate from 27 Oct 2026. The engine counted every point as a "month" and averaged them all, so a base window ending Dec 2026 passed the 10-of-12 check on two weeks of daily rates and its average leaned on those days. `stress.monthly_averages()` now gives one value a month: the monthly average when the series has one, else the average of that month's daily rates (`usdinr_d` says which points are daily) when there are at least 10. `today_scenario()` counts those months; a page is built only when `usdinr_source` is `FinLit usdinr` (since 13 Nov 2026 the stop-gap is gone, SL-9). The latest-quarter check uses the same monthly values. Asian Paints' numbers do not change (the handover's `check_payload.py` matches the company, settings, every scenario and the back-test).
- **The engine's warnings stay in the log**: `today_inputs.warnings` is no longer in the page payload.
- **The nightly queue reaches every company.** Each run started again from the largest company (only pages built *today* were skipped), so the same few dozen were rebuilt every day and the rest never got a page; and a run that stopped at its 40-minute cap exited "ok", so the job did not run again until the next night. Now the order is: no page yet, then pages whose company reported a newer quarter, then the oldest pages (recent failures last). Each run writes `stress:last_run` in Redis, and the supervisor's `stress` probe (`jobs/probes.py`, `service.has_work`) is true when the last run left companies it never reached, so the job runs again 5 minutes later (`min_gap_min=5`) until every company is built that day. Time cap, SLA and retries are unchanged.
- **A bad provider answer is not "not covered".** Statements without an income statement made `build_pack` raise `NotCovered` (deleting the stored page) while the bad answer stayed in Redis for a day. It is now `NotReady`, and the cached copies are dropped (`service.forget_premium`) so the next try asks again. A provider failure is never cached (only status 200 is stored); the job and the visit share the same Redis keys.
- **On demand (router).** A failed build of a company is not started again for `RETRY_AFTER_S` (120 s) however often the page polls; the reader gets the same plain answer (404 for not covered, else 503). A stored page older than `PAGE_MAX_AGE_H` (30 h), or behind a newer reported quarter (and over 6 h old), is served at once and rebuilt behind the reader.
- **Financial companies are out of the universe up front (SL-5).** `service.coverage_reason()` adds NSE's sector of the symbol or of its industry (Financial Services: banks, NBFCs, insurers, capital-market firms, fintech) and the site's sector or industry name to the screener's `is_bank` flag, so quick picks, warm-up, the nightly queue and peers never include an insurer, AMC or exchange, and their 404 carries the financial sentence.
- **Peer choice.** `/peer` orders the covered peers: stored page and same quarter, same quarter, stored page, any (`page.default_peer(..., stored=)`, which adds `stored` to `{symbol, same_quarter}`). A chosen peer with no page gets a background build (at most `WARM_AT_ONCE` at a time) so the page's polling finds it. `peer_details` lists each covered peer with its `base_end` and `stored`.
- **/run and /compare** no longer send the model's workings: each company's `assumptions` is dropped and `value` keeps only `move_pct` (`router._public`).
- **Today's inputs table** shows a dash and "(no reading in the base window)" for a series with no reading in the window, instead of failing the build.


## The 13 Nov 2026 fixes (task #79, SL-9 to SL-12)

- **One close, one set of inputs (SL-9).** Every page is built on the latest completed close: the newest close the nightly job stored for the large reference companies (RELIANCE, TCS, HDFCBANK, INFY, ICICIBANK), never later than the last session that is over (before 16:00 IST and until the evening refresh, the previous trading day). The share price is the company's own close on that day (`price_daily`), never a mid-session price; `as_of` and `company.price_as_of` are that close. The "Today" inputs (Brent, the FBIL rupee series, inflation, repo, GDP, the EIA outlook) are one snapshot per close, cut to points on or before it, stored in `stress_inputs` and used by every page of that close. A stored page from an older close, another model version or another rupee source is never served: it is rebuilt on the current close, from its stored statements when the company has reported nothing newer (seconds, no provider call). The nightly run rebuilds those pages first.
- **Internal notes (SL-11).** `company.notes` and `company.exposure_notes` are no longer in the payload; the build logs them. `today_inputs.warnings` was already left out.
- **Real shocks (SL-10).** The company's closes are the long weekly history, the chart's price-MAX section (read through the store) and the daily closes, put together from 1 Jan 2018 to the latest close; when the site holds nothing before 2022 they are fetched once from the provider and kept in `price_history_long`. The Nifty 50's older years are fetched a year at a time and kept (`stress:nifty:past`); the recent years come from the site's own index history.
- **Small fixes (SL-12).** The i button is never upper-cased by its label; text boxes are 16 px on phones; on a phone "Share feedback" (site-wide) is a small round icon that steps aside while the page scrolls down; Compare two says "about the same" only under half a point of EPS, otherwise names the company that takes the bigger hit.
- **The team's check (`stress_check.py`).** Its default close is the screener's `as_of`, which is a calendar date (it read 14 Nov, a Saturday, on 14 Nov). Run it with `--close <latest trading day>` (for example `--close 2026-11-13` over a weekend).
