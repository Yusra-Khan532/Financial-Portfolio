 1. Product objective and scope
 Build a dedicated Macro & Sector Outlook section on every individual stock/company analysis page. The feature
 evaluates how the current Indian macroeconomic environment may support or pressure the sector in which a company
 operates.

 The module must answer: (a) Is the macro environment favourable for this sector? (b) Which indicators are driving the
 assessment? (c) What external factors should investors monitor? (d) How has the outlook changed over time?

 Out of scope: company-specific financial health, stock valuation, price targets, buy/sell recommendations and
 technical signals. The Macro Score must remain separate from Fundamental Score and Technical Score.

 Core architecture: one shared macro dataset → normalised indicator signals → sector-specific sensitivity profiles →
 sector scores → company-page presentation.


 2. Placement and user experience
 Recommended placement: after Company Overview / Business Profile and before or alongside Fundamental,
 Technical and Valuation analysis. The section should be available on every supported company page.

 Section heading: MACRO & SECTOR OUTLOOK.

 Component A – Macro Backdrop Summary: compact horizontal summary showing sentiment, mapped sector,
 score out of 100, two most influential drivers and a “How it works” link.

 Example: MACRO BACKDROP | Strong Headwind | Real Estate | 22/100 | Rate hikes (−), Credit (+). Values are
 illustrative only.

 Component B – Sector Impact: comparative table showing sector, horizontal score bar, score, sentiment and top
 reasons. Highlight the currently viewed company’s sector. Sort descending by score by default; allow expansion to all
 supported sectors. Include a visible midpoint marker at 50.

 Component C – Indicator Signals: grid grouped by Inflation, Monetary Policy, Credit & Banking, Economic Growth,
 Currency and Commodities. Each tile shows latest value, previous value/change, direction, normalised signal,
 observation date and source.

 Component D – Sector Interpretation: concise explanation generated from the largest actual score contributions.
 Show both supporting and adverse factors, plus relevant indicators to monitor.

 Component E – How It Works: expandable methodology covering score meaning, indicators, normalisation,
 sensitivities, formula, sources, update frequency and limitations.

  Score                    Sentiment

  80–100                   Strong Tailwind

  65–79                    Tailwind

  45–64                    Neutral

  25–44                    Headwind

  0–24                     Strong Headwind

 Visual design: restrained colours, strong readability, compact rows, subtle badges, neutral midpoint, responsive
 desktop/mobile layout. Suggested palette: dark green for strong positive, muted green for positive, grey for neutral,
 muted red for negative and dark red for strong negative. Avoid excessive cards and colour saturation.

 Create a central ingestion and scoring service. Do not fetch macroeconomic data independently for each company
 page. Store raw readings centrally and calculate sector scores once per data/model snapshot; company pages
 consume cached results.

 Data validation: check presence, valid observation/publication dates, units, acceptable ranges, duplicates, freshness
 and source revisions. Status values should include Available, Stale, Missing, Invalid and Revised.

 Publication-date rule: distinguish observation date from publication date. The engine must not use information before
 it was publicly available at the score’s as-of timestamp. This avoids look-ahead bias in historical scores.

 Missing-data rule: never silently replace missing values with zero or neutral. Exclude unavailable inputs, calculate
 coverage and apply the minimum-coverage rules in Section 6. Serve the last valid snapshot with a stale-data warning
 if a refresh fails.


 4. Indicator normalisation engine
 Raw macro indicators use different units and directions. Convert each active indicator into a normalised signal
 between −1 and +1 before applying sector sensitivities. Higher values are not universally better; direction depends on
 the indicator and sector context.

 Two-part signal: calculate a Level Signal and a Momentum Signal, each capped to [−1, +1].
            Indicator Signal = (Level Signal + Momentum Signal) / 2

 Level signal evaluates whether the current reading is favourable relative to configured thresholds, target ranges or
 historical benchmarks. Momentum signal evaluates whether the indicator is improving or deteriorating over a
 configured comparison period.

 Supported normalisation methods: threshold/range-based, historical percentile, percentage-change,
 rate-of-change/momentum and capped z-score methods. Each indicator must specify its method, lookback window,
 direction, thresholds, units and treatment of extreme values in configuration.

 Examples: inflation may be favourable when easing from an elevated level but adverse if rising sharply; credit growth
 may be positive up to a healthy range but excessive growth can indicate overheating; repo-rate changes must be
 distinguished from the current repo-rate level.


 5. Sector sensitivity engine
 Create a configurable sector-indicator sensitivity matrix. Each sector has a sensitivity coefficient for each relevant
 indicator, between −1.00 and +1.00. Coefficients represent expected direction and strength of sector sensitivity; they
 are model assumptions and do not prove causality.

 Sensitivity profiles must be stored in the database/configuration service, not hardcoded in frontend logic. Include
 effective dates, active status, model version and audit trail. Validate that sector-specific drivers are economically
 plausible. For banks, distinguish asset yields, deposit repricing, funding costs and credit quality rather than assuming
 rate hikes are always positive or negative.

 For diversified companies, use segment-level exposures where reliable revenue contribution data exists. Avoid
 applying one broad sector coefficient to materially different business models.


 6. Final Macro Score calculation
 Step 1 – Indicator signal: compute the average of level and momentum signals, each capped between −1 and +1.
             IndicatorSignal■ = (LevelSignal■ + MomentumSignal■) / 2

 Step 2 – Sector contribution: multiply indicator signal by sector sensitivity. If a separate weight is configured, include
 it explicitly.
          Contribution■ = IndicatorSignal■ × Sensitivity■ × Weight■

 Step 3 – Aggregate: sum valid contributions. Use a consistent weight normalisation policy. Recommended: divide the
 sum by the sum of absolute active weights for the valid indicators, then scale to the model’s configured signal range.
 The chosen method must be fixed, versioned and documented.

 Step 4 – Convert to score: apply a bounded tanh transformation.
          MacroScore = 50 + 50 × tanh(WeightedMacroSignal / K)

 K is a configurable normalisation factor. An initial trial value of 3 may be used for calibration, not assumed correct.
 Score is bounded between 0 and 100; a zero aggregate signal produces 50.

 Important implementation clarification: the earlier illustrative formula used a simple sum divided by 3. For
 production, the engineering team must implement one explicitly defined aggregation method (including weight
 normalisation) and validate the resulting distribution on historical data. Do not mix raw sums and normalised weighted
 averages.

 Coverage: calculate valid active weight divided by total active weight. Recommended initial minimum coverage is
 70%, subject to validation. Also define critical indicators per sector; a sector must not receive a confident score if a
 critical input is missing even when aggregate coverage passes. If coverage fails, return “Insufficient Data” rather than
 a fabricated score.

 Top drivers: rank valid indicators by absolute contribution. Display the two largest, with sign, latest value and unit. If
 contributions are close, use a deterministic tie-break rule such as configured priority, then freshness.

 Sentiment bands: 80–100 Strong Tailwind; 65–79 Tailwind; 45–64 Neutral; 25–44 Headwind; 0–24 Strong Headwind.


 7. Company-sector mapping
 Maintain a standard sector master and company-to-sector mapping table. Initial coverage should include: private
 banks, PSU banks, NBFCs, housing finance, insurance, real estate, automobiles, auto ancillaries, FMCG, consumer
 durables, cement, capital goods, infrastructure, metals & mining, specialty chemicals, paints, pharmaceuticals,
 hospitals, IT services, telecom, aviation, hotels & QSR, oil marketing, upstream oil & gas, power generation, power
 transmission, renewable energy, agri inputs, textiles, logistics, media & entertainment, paper, sugar, fertilisers and
 diversified.

 Mapping must be editable by authorised administrators and retain effective dates. Every listed company should have a
 primary sector mapping. Where segment revenue data is available, use a revenue-weighted company score:
          CompanyMacroScore = Σ (SegmentRevenueWeight■ × SectorMacroScore■)

 Segment weights must total 100% (or be normalised with an explicit rule). If segment data is unavailable, use the
 primary business sector and disclose that limitation. Do not invent segment weights.


 8. Historical macro intelligence
 Persist immutable score snapshots so users can view score history and reproduce past calculations. UI ranges: 1M,
 3M, 6M, 1Y and All. Show current score, changes over selected periods, line chart, 50 midpoint reference and dates.

 Store score snapshot, input observations, contributions, sensitivity configuration version, model version and as-of
 timestamp. If source data is revised, retain the original published snapshot. Optionally calculate a separately labelled
 revised historical series; never silently overwrite history.

 sector_id, company_id and snapshot_date, and uniqueness constraints to prevent duplicate observations/snapshots.
 Preserve source provenance and configuration versions.

 11. Automation and refresh schedule
 Recommended cadence: USD/INR and Brent daily; repo rate on RBI announcements; CPI, WPI and IIP monthly after
 official release; GDP quarterly; credit/deposit growth on official release; unemployment as published. Refresh
 schedules must follow source publication calendars rather than assumed dates.

 Pipeline: fetch → validate → persist raw observation → detect revisions → normalise signals → apply sector
 sensitivities → calculate scores → identify drivers → generate explanation → save immutable snapshot →
 invalidate/refresh cache.

 Trigger recalculation when a relevant new observation is published or when an authorised administrator activates a

 12. Admin panel requirements
 • Add, edit, deactivate indicators and configure units, frequency, source and normalisation method.

 • Update sector-indicator sensitivity coefficients, weights and critical-indicator flags.

 • Configure score bands, normalisation factor K and minimum coverage rules.

 • View missing, stale, invalid and revised source data.

 • Manually trigger ingestion, validation and score recalculation.

 • Edit company-sector and segment mapping.

 • Inspect indicator contributions and score calculation logs.

 • Review model versions, effective dates and historical snapshots.

 • Audit every configuration change with user, timestamp, old value and new value.

 Admin configuration must be role-protected, validated and versioned. A model change should create a new effective
 version; it must not silently alter previously published snapshots.


 13. Error handling, security and performance
 Error cases: missing data, stale data, source outage, invalid units, duplicate observations, revised data, newly listed
 companies, diversified companies without segment data, insufficient sector coverage, extreme readings, conflicting
 indicators and failed score jobs.

 Fallback: serve the last successful snapshot with its original timestamp and a visible stale-data warning. Do not
 display a stale score as current.

 Security: only authorised admins can change model settings. Public endpoints are read-only. Validate inputs, apply
 rate limits where appropriate, store secrets securely and audit administrative changes.

 Performance: cache sector scores and indicator payloads; do not call external macro sources during company page
 rendering. Keep the macro engine independent from fundamental and technical scoring services.

 16. Future enhancements and launch checklist
 Future enhancements (not MVP blockers): macro score versus sector-index performance; macro event impact;
 sector rotation intelligence; scenario analysis (e.g. repo ±25 bps, crude ±10%, CPI +1 percentage point); and
 threshold-based macro alerts.

 Launch checklist:

 • All data sources approved and documented; licensing/usage rights checked.

 • Observation date and publication date stored separately.

 • Normalisation direction and thresholds reviewed by product/data owner.

 • Sector sensitivities reviewed and backtested; model limitations disclosed.

 • Aggregation and missing-data policy finalised and unit-tested.

 • Historical snapshots immutable and reproducible.

 • Company mapping and segment weights reviewed.

 • UI displays as-of date, coverage, source and stale-data warnings.

 • Admin access, audit trail, monitoring and rollback tested.

 • Investment-advice disclaimer and methodology explanation reviewed.


 Final product outcome: Every supported stock page should connect Macroeconomic Data → Indicator Signals
 → Sector Sensitivities → Sector Macro Score → Investor Interpretation. The feature should be scalable,
 transparent, explainable and independently maintained from Fundamental and Technical scoring.


 Disclaimer: The Macro & Sector Outlook is an informational model of sector-level macroeconomic conditions. It is not a company-specific
 assessment, forecast, or investment recommendation. Sensitivity assumptions and model outputs are subject to uncertainty and must be
 validated.
